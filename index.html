Fetal Sexual Differentiation

Practice the connections among genetic sex, gonadal differentiation, internal reproductive ducts, and external genital development.

Learning objectives

Genetic sex Explain how fertilization establishes an XX, XY, or other sex-chromosome complement.
Gonadal sex Connect SRY-driven testicular differentiation with subsequent hormone production.
Phenotypic development Predict internal and external anatomy from the presence, absence, or action of testosterone, DHT, and anti-Müllerian hormone.

Important anatomical clarification

  • The Wolffian ducts do not form the testes. Testes develop from the indifferent gonads. Testosterone supports differentiation of the Wolffian ducts into the epididymides, vasa deferentia, seminal vesicles, and ejaculatory ducts.
  • The Müllerian ducts do not form the ovaries. Ovaries develop from the indifferent gonads. Müllerian ducts form the uterine tubes, uterus, cervix, and upper portion of the vagina.
  • These developmental events overlap in time. The matching activity emphasizes the relationship between each signal or condition and its outcome rather than implying a strict sequence.

Testicular testosterone supports Wolffian duct differentiation, Sertoli-cell AMH causes Müllerian duct regression, and DHT supports masculinization of the external genitalia and prostate.

Pathway overview

XY fertilization Usually includes SRY on the Y chromosome
→
Testes Sertoli cells produce AMH; Leydig cells produce testosterone
→
Typical male pathway Wolffian structures are retained, Müllerian ducts regress, and DHT masculinizes the external genitalia
XX fertilization SRY is usually absent
→
Ovaries No fetal testes, so there is no testicular AMH or high local testicular testosterone
→
Typical female pathway Müllerian structures develop, Wolffian ducts regress, and external genitalia follow the female-typical pathway

High-yield summary

Signal Major developmental effect If absent or unable to act
SRY and associated testis-determining pathways Promote differentiation of the indifferent gonad into testes. The gonad generally follows an ovarian pathway if ovarian-promoting pathways function.
Testosterone Supports Wolffian duct differentiation into male internal duct structures. Wolffian ducts regress.
DHT Supports development of the penis, scrotum, and prostate. External genitalia are not fully masculinized.
AMH Causes Müllerian duct regression. Müllerian structures can develop.
Internal and external anatomy can differ Testosterone and DHT have related but distinct roles.
Hormone production is not enough The target tissue must also contain a functional receptor and signaling pathway.
Chromosomes are not the entire pathway Gonadal development, hormone synthesis, hormone conversion, and tissue response all contribute to phenotype.
Fetal Sexual Differentiation Practice

Educational terminology distinguishes chromosomal, gonadal, and phenotypic sex. Differences of sex development may involve chromosomes, gonadal development, hormone synthesis, or hormone action.

This activity is for instruction and is not a diagnostic tool. Individual clinical presentations vary, and patient-centered terminology should be used.
